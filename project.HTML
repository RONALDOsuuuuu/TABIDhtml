All Facts About Bee

Bees are among the most important insects on Earth because they pollinate many of the plants that produce our food and support natural ecosystems. There are over 20,000 known species of bees worldwide, and they are found on every continent except Antarctica.

What is a bee

A bee is a flying insect belonging to the superfamily Apoidea. Unlike wasps, bees mainly feed on nectar (for energy) and pollen (for protein).

Types of bees

The three main types of honey bees in a colony are: Queen Bee Only one queen in most colonies. Lays all the eggs. Can lay up to 2,000 eggs per day during peak season. Lives 2-5 years. Worker Bees Female bees that do not reproduce. Clean the hive, collect nectar and pollen, care for larvae, make honey, and defend the hive. Live about 4-6 weeks during summer and several months during winter. Drone Bees Male bees. Their main job is to mate with a queen. They do not sting. Usually die after mating or are removed from the hive before winter. Bee anatomy A bee has: Head Thorax Abdomen Two antennae Five eyes (2 compound eyes + 3 simple eyes) Six legs Two pairs of wings A long tongue (proboscis) for drinking nectar A pollen basket on the hind legs (worker bees) How bees make honey Worker bees collect nectar from flowers. Nectar is stored in a special "honey stomach." The nectar is passed to other worker bees. Enzymes break down the sugars. The bees store the nectar in honeycomb cells. They fan their wings to evaporate water. The thickened nectar becomes honey. The honeycomb is sealed with wax. Bee life cycle Bees go through complete metamorphosis: Egg Larva Pupa Adult Communication Bees communicate using: The waggle dance, which tells other bees the direction and distance of food. Chemical scents called pheromones. Vibrations and sounds inside the hive. Importance of bees Bees: Pollinate about 75% of the world's leading food crops to some extent. Help produce fruits, vegetables, nuts, and seeds. Increase crop yields and quality. Support wild plants and biodiversity. Without bees, many foods would become much scarcer and more expensive. What do bees eat? Adults: Nectar and honey. Larvae: Pollen mixed with nectar. Queen larvae: Royal jelly, produced by young worker bees. Bee stings Only female bees can sting. Honey bees usually die after stinging mammals because the barbed stinger gets stuck. Many other bee species can sting more than once. Bee venom can cause pain, swelling, and in some people, severe allergic reactions. Amazing bee facts Bees can recognize human faces. A honey bee beats its wings about 200 times per second. A bee can fly up to 24 km/h (15 mph). Worker bees visit 50-100 flowers on a single trip. It takes about 12 bees their entire lifetimes to produce one teaspoon of honey. Honey stored by bees can remain edible for many years if kept properly. Bee habitats Bees live in many places: Hollow trees Underground burrows Rock crevices Wood cavities Man-made hives Gardens, forests, grasslands, deserts, and farms Threats to bees Bee populations are declining due to: Habitat loss Pesticides Climate change Diseases and parasites (such as the Varroa mite) Poor nutrition from reduced plant diversity How to help bees Plant native flowering plants. Avoid harmful pesticides. Leave some wild areas for nesting. Provide a shallow water source. Support local beekeepers. Grow flowers that bloom throughout the year.
